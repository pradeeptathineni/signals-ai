# Research

Research when evidence can improve correctness, reduce material uncertainty, or change a decision. Do not research to decorate an answer or postpone implementation.

## Find evidence

- Start with the question, decision, and required confidence. Define what evidence would change the outcome.
- Prefer current specifications, standards, first-party documentation, source code, release notes, and original data.
- Use reputable secondary sources to compare interpretations or fill gaps the primary source does not cover.
- Prefer direct evidence over popularity. Adoption matters when interoperability, maintenance, or ecosystem support is part of the decision.
- Check publication and update dates, product surface, software version, and deprecation status.
- For volatile facts, verify at the point of use and record a review date.

## Synthesize

- Separate what a source states from what you infer or recommend.
- Reconcile conflicts by authority, scope, currency, and directness. Report unresolved conflicts that could affect the result.
- Record enough provenance for another maintainer to refresh durable knowledge: title, stable URL, version or revision when available, and review date.
- Quote sparingly. Summarize the rule or evidence that changes the repository.
- Check license and attribution requirements before copying any artifact. Link to proprietary material instead of vendoring it.

## Stop

Stop when the decision is supported at the required confidence and another source is unlikely to change it. More sources are not stronger evidence when they repeat the same upstream claim.

Research output should make action easier: the relevant facts, their authority and freshness, remaining uncertainty, and the decision they support.
